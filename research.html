Active projects

From research relationship to research output

Concierge Clinical Evidence Service (CCES)

DBMI · CCAI · ENACT Network
On-demand evidence synthesis for primary care clinicians, built on the national ENACT clinical data network.
CCES answers a clinician's specific patient-context question by combining structured EHR data with literature synthesis — the kind of point-of-care evidence lookup that's normally too slow to use during a visit. It draws on ENACT's federated network of clinical data across participating health systems, with NLP infrastructure contributed by Pitt's PittNAIL lab.

Ambient AI & the Patient-Provider Relationship

Qualitative study · Dr. Bridges
A qualitative study of how ambient documentation tools change clinical workflow and the patient-provider relationship across Pennsylvania family medicine practices.

Multi-Agent LLM for Secondary Headache Diagnosis

Dr. Wang, with DBMI & UT Southwestern
A multi-agent LLM system — one orchestrator, seven specialist agents — for flagging secondary headache in primary care, tested on 90 expert-validated cases (F1 = 0.605).
Agents cover thunderclap onset, meningismus, papilledema, temporal arteritis, systemic illness, focal neurologic deficits, and first-or-worst headache presentation. The MERLIN prototype is publicly available as a Docker image.

Agent-Based Modeling of Influenza Vaccination (FRED)

Zimmerman & Williams · School of Public Health
Simulating population-level flu burden under different vaccination strategies, using Pitt's FRED agent-based epidemic platform.
FRED represents every person in a region individually, inside real household, school, and workplace networks — letting Zimmerman and Williams model questions no clinical trial can answer directly, like the effect of uneven household vaccination or two-dose regimens for older adults. 10+ FRED-based publications since 2010.

4 Pillars™ Practice Transformation Program

Dept. of Family Medicine
A home-grown, evidence-based model for closing the gap between what clinicians know and what happens in a visit — organized around access, communication, systems, and motivation.
Developed to protect patients from vaccine-preventable disease, the program was tested in before-and-after studies, then randomized cluster trials, then put into clinical use across UPMC — and found cost-effective. 14 peer-reviewed publications; National Publication Award from the National Adult and Influenza Immunization Summit. For PITTCARE, it's a template for how quality-improvement infrastructure already trusted by clinicians can carry new intelligence-technology into practice.

Critical Appraisal in the Setting of AI — Course

Pitt Med Year 2 · Dr. Maier
A required second-year course teaching evidence-based critical appraisal applied specifically to AI tools — one of the first in the country to require the national STFM AiM-PC curriculum.
All enrolled students complete the first two modules of the STFM Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning for Primary Care (AiM-PC) curriculum. This satisfies the Education and Workforce domain of the STFM Family Medicine AI Centers of Excellence recognition program, and gives every Pitt med student an early, structured framework for evaluating AI in clinical practice — not just using it.
